# Audit — August 2026

A point-in-time review of what this mod could become and whether it could get there, taken
against commit `ab79427` (v0.8.3). Twenty-six agents read the source, the decompiled KSA corpus
and the tooling, and every load-bearing architectural claim was then handed to an adversary told
to refute it.

**This is a snapshot, not a living document.** It records what was true on the day. Where a
finding here has been acted on, the fix belongs in the code and the reasoning in the file it
concerns — not in edits to this one. `docs/BLOCKED-ON-KSA.md` is the living companion: what the
game does not allow, rechecked on every KSA update.

The ranked list at the end is the part to work from.

---

Nothing in this report was flown. Every claim below is a claim about the source, computed constants, or a tool that was run read-only. Per the repo's own rule, that means the behaviour items are diagnoses, not fixes.

## TRACK 1 — What to build

### The ones that are genuinely different and genuinely reachable

**Terrain masking / line-of-sight.** The single highest-leverage change on the list. The radar currently sees through the planet — `ThreatModel.TryAssess` is range, cone and speed, over ground truth for every loaded vehicle. KSA gives you more than expected and you write no marching code: `TerrainImpactFinder.TryFind` is a public static coarse-march-plus-24-bisections solver, generic over the one-method public `ITerrainTrajectory`, with a public `ParabolicTrajectory` that degenerates to a straight line at zero acceleration; `Ray.Raycast(BoundingSphere3D)` gives the planet-limb test and is what the game itself uses for solar occlusion. It also lands free in a second place: `ThreatModel.InSensorVolume` is already the command-link uplink gate, so a target dropping behind a ridge cuts guidance to rounds already flying at it with no new call site. **Unmeasured:** heightmap resolution (it will resolve mountains and curvature, not hedgerows) and per-sample cost. Sample a dozen tracks per frame, not per round per sub-step.

**Target RCS, Doppler notch, ground-clutter floor.** Three fields on `SensorProfile` and three comparisons in `ThreatModel.TryAssess`. All pure `Sim/`, all testable the moment they exist, all making detection depend on what the target *is* or *is doing* — which nothing in the mod does today. RCS is the substrate that makes chaff, decoys and jammers legible later. The notch has a real tension worth stating rather than burying: CLAUDE.md records CPA-not-closing-speed as an explicit requirement, and a notch bites exactly the crossing geometry that protects. Ship it as a per-sensor field defaulting to zero, not as a change to how the 1RS1 behaves. One trap: `Ui.cs` enumerates the sensor sliders by hand, so any new `SensorProfile` field ships unreachable by a player and no test notices.

**The operator cluster — main-viewport takeover, then HUD symbology, zoom, two reticules.** The whole operator overlay is currently invisible unless a second camera window is open and picked, and `docs/BLOCKED-ON-KSA.md` already records that secondary viewports render no sky, terrain or atmosphere. The workaround is named in that file and no code does it: take viewport 0. `Program.cs`'s `OnFrameViewports` runs the controllers *before* `OnDrawUiViewports`, so a GUI-hook camera write survives the frame — the same ordering `KsaWorld` already relies on. Zoom has a verified asymmetry to exploit: `Camera.SetFieldOfView` applies with **no clamp**, while `ChangeFieldOfView` clamps to 15–120°, so 3° (~20×) is reachable — but the player's zoom keys route through the clamping one, so the mod must rewrite FOV every frame. Build the takeover once and correctly; the gunner's sight and the missile-eye camera both depend on it.

**IFF and teams panel.** Not new capability — a complete, tested subsystem with no way in. `Sim/Iff.cs` ships with `IffTests.cs`, `Radar.Scan` classifies every contact and stores allegiance on the track, `DefenceBattery` gates fire on it in three places, and `Config.Iff` / `Config.TeamNames` have **zero write sites anywhere outside `Config.cs`**. The mod has behaved, in every session anyone has played, as if teams did not exist. One tree node in `Ui.cs`. Carry the verified trap: `Radar.TeamOf` matches team names as case-insensitive *substrings* of the craft's display name, longest wins — so a panel presenting this as team assignment will put "Redstone" on team "Red".

**Timed airburst — and it is cheaper than the investigation first claimed.** The adversarial pass killed the "needs a `FuseMode` enum" framing. What survived: `Aimpoint.AtPoint` exists, `SampleTarget` serves a Point aimpoint a stationary zero-velocity `TargetState` every frame with no world lookup, the proximity fuse solves closest approach analytically against whatever `TargetState` arrives, `BallisticLead.TrySolve` already computes the lead and `AimPointEcl` already calls it, and `Detonate` falls through to a positional blast sweep that never consults `TargetRef`. A muzzle-set airburst at a solved point is roughly one expression in `FireGun` (`Aimpoint.OnVehicle` → `Aimpoint.AtPoint(lead, burstRadius)`, reusing the already-computed lead) plus a `MunitionProfile` instance. A `Contact` fuse mode would be a *second representation of shipped behaviour* — zero `FuseRadius` already means contact and is pinned by two tests. Only a burst on a pure timer with **no** aimpoint is genuinely new machinery. This is also the cheapest way to flush out `Aimpoint.AtPoint`, which has never executed in production.

**Cluster / submunition dispenser.** Confirmed under adversarial review, and cheaper than proposed: **no `IProjectile` change is needed.** `DefenceBattery` is the sole author of `_rounds`, and the dispense instant already exists as a case in `UpdateRounds` — the `Detonated` branch, with the parent's post-step position and velocity and `Radar.Tracks` all in hand. The reverse loop is safe to append into. It is a child-munition key on `MunitionProfile` plus ~10 lines, with one hard constraint verified complete: children must carry negative tube numbers (or ≥ `TubeCount`), or `Magazine.IsOccupied` keeps the parent's tube locked forever and `SyncRoundBodies` trips the double-booking warning. The gun path already establishes the negative-numbering convention.

**Directed-energy laser — real, but large, and third in queue.** KSA is more generous than expected: `Part.RayCastEgo` is public and watertight per-triangle against the real mesh (it is the game's own mouse part-picking), `<PowerConsumer>`/`<Battery>`/`<Generator>` are declarable from a mod's GameData XML, and the engine's own updater gates `Active` off `LightIsActive` and zeroes it when the batteries flat — so brown-out is free. Two verified traps: there is no damage below destruction, so burn-through must be a mod-side integral ending in a binary kill; and the per-part heat glow is unusable — `FxTemperature` only exists on parts carrying `<PartModelDynamic>` (tanks and propulsion), and for those the updater clamps `EmissivityFraction` to `[-1, 0]` every step, so a mod's "hot" write is wiped on the next tick on precisely the parts that have the channel. The cost is a parallel discrete-lifecycle in `Ksa/` — the cannon, which was the *cheap* case because a shell is an `IProjectile`, still landed across nine files with ~30 touchpoints. Do it after per-craft batteries.

### The reskins — the Pantsir with different numbers

Say these out loud and stop costing them:

- **Standalone CIWS mount, heavy MG, quad AA, autocannon, chain gun, remote weapon station.** All one shape: a gun-only `LauncherProfile`. `UpdateGunFireControl` is already deliberately not gated on the missile channel. Cheapest real content on the list, and not a new capability.
- **Anti-tank gun, railgun-as-a-round, RPG, recoilless rifle.** `Slug` with a bigger `LaunchSpeed`. The part that would make them weapons — penetration, armour, angle of attack — is downstream of damage below destruction, which KSA does not have.
- **Hit-to-kill exo interceptor.** The 57E6 with `FuseRadius` near zero and no blast. It works because the fuse solves closest approach analytically per sub-step and clamps to `[0, h]` — resolution is not the limit at any closing speed, guidance is. Note `Detonate` scores the intended kill on `MissDistance <= LethalRadius + target MeanRadius`, so on a hit-to-kill round the target's own size is doing most of the work.
- **Torpedo.** A `MunitionProfile` with a small `DragK` and `NeutralDensityRatio` near 840. The surface-crossing stability worry does not stand — `mediumDensityRatio` is sampled once per frame and passed as a constant to every sub-step, so a frame is integrated wholly in air or wholly in water, and the explicit-Euler drag condition is an order of magnitude inside the sign-flip threshold. The real cost of a crossing is metres of overshoot, not divergence.
- **Multi-stage rounds** (a boost schedule), **nuclear warheads** (two numbers, kills are binary), **IR / active-radar / semi-active seeker variants** (a display string until a signature model exists), **automatic grenade launcher** (flak at 400 m/s), **depth charge** (a Slug with a time fuse, no underwater targets), **free-fall bomb** (no aircraft in KSA).
- **Phased array radar.** The mod already *is* one. Simultaneous perfect tracking of everything in the field of view with no scan penalty and no revisit interval is an AESA's defining behaviour, and `Radar.Scan` hands it to every sensor for free. The buildable thing is the *mechanically scanned set* — the penalty, not the capability — and it is cheap because `RadarSpinRad` already advances off `SearchRadarRpm` on simulated time and is currently fed only to the mesh.
- **Laser rangefinder** (range is already ground truth to double precision), **low-light mode** (one boolean), **IFF interrogation / transponders** (the string match already is an IFF model), **multi-band radar / frequency agility** (counters to a detection model that does not exist), **reticule style gallery**, **beam-riding / CLOS**.

### Blocked, and honestly so

- **No damage below destruction.** `Universe.DestroyVehicleFromEvent` and six destruction causes, none of them ours. Everything is kill / near miss / miss. Do not design a damage bar.
- **No AI, anywhere.** A grep of the whole 43 MB corpus for autopilot / VehicleAI / BehaviorTree returns nothing. Nothing in the game will ever decide to dispense chaff — every countermeasure must be mod-driven on the target craft (`LauncherPart.Find` already scans an arbitrary vehicle for a registered part Id, so the mechanism exists).
- **No particle or trail access.** Chaff, flares and smoke all render as clusters of gizmo spheres. Accept that or do not build them.
- **No render hook.** StarMap patches three UI/frame methods and nothing in the render path. A real thermal render is impossible; the one image lever, the tonemapper, is global, needs a renderer rebuild with a device wait-idle, and has no false-colour entry. A styled IR overlay is the honest version. Add this to `docs/BLOCKED-ON-KSA.md` with the evidence.
- **Multiplayer is a skeleton** — join/chat/replicated-objects. There is no armed-craft-versus-armed-craft context to build for.
- **RWR, EMCON, anti-radiation homing, datalink** are blocked on the *mod*, not the game: one battery per world, and it is the player's. An RWR would warn you that you are illuminating yourself. Build them the day per-craft batteries land, not before.
- **Unestablished:** whether a mod's write to a `Battery` charge survives the engine's double-buffered module update. The read path is unambiguously public; the mutable accessor is public but named `GetModuleAndAllMutableStatesForInitialization` and sits beside a separate `StateUpdater.GetNewState`. This only decides whether a railgun draws from the craft or from its own store — it blocks nothing.

## TRACK 2 — Architecture

### Data-only (profile entry plus art; nothing blocks)

| Candidate | Note |

Also data-only and worth noticing: **the optical head as a real sensor.** `OpticOnTarget` is a settled flag on a rate-limited `PointingDrive` consumed by exactly one caller — `Sight.Draw` — and by nothing in fire control. Giving it a `SensorProfile` and making its settled flag a release gate needs no new type, and it **creates** the second sensor instance that cueing, LOS and every EW item currently assume.

### One new abstraction — and which are earned

**Earned today, two real instances:**

1. **Weapon channels on `LauncherProfile`.** Channels are encoded as field-name *prefixes*: `Munition`/`Tubes`/`MagazineDepth`/`SalvoSpacing`/`ReloadSeconds`/`LaunchAlongTube`/`LaunchLoft` + `PodsMarker`/`PodPivotFromTurret`/`PodReferenceElevationRad` against `GunMunition`/`GunMuzzles`/`GunAmmo`/`GunRoundsPerMinute`/`GunBurstRounds`/`GunBurstGapSeconds`/`GunMinRange`/`GunMaxRange`/`GunReloadSeconds` + `GunsMarker`/`GunPivotFromTurret`/`GunReferenceElevationRad`. ~11 fields per channel, two disjoint families. A third channel costs a third family. Two real instances ship — build the list before the laser or railgun forces it.
2. **Per-channel elevation and per-channel `IsLaid`.** `Turret` holds one `ElevationRad` and one `CommandElevationRad`; `DefenceBattery` hands the identical value to `PodPose` and `GunPose` under the comment "one turret, one solution". Two real trunnions exist and `checkswept.py` already sweeps them as independent bodies. This has a **live** consequence, not a cosmetic one — see defects.
3. **Sensors as a list on `LauncherProfile`.** `LauncherProfile.Sensor` is a single `required string`; `Arsenal.Sensors` being a list is irrelevant while a launcher can name one of them. This *is* a registry change, contrary to the sensors track's claim. The second instance is real hardware already modelled, pivoted, rate-limited, aimed and drawn — the optic. Make it sense first, then widen.

**Not earned — one instance or zero. Wait.**

- `FuseMode` enum (one mode today; and `Contact` is already a data value, so an enum member would duplicate shipped behaviour).
- A shared dispersion field (one real consumer; flak and MLRS are candidates, not instances).
- A false-contact registry (chaff and its decoy variant are one instance — build both or neither).
- A continuous-effect sibling to `IProjectile` (zero instances).
- A third rotation level (zero instances — see below).
- Any general seeker/fuse/guidance plug-in framework.

**Already shipped speculative, and the pattern to stop repeating:** `Tube.Direction` (zero users; neither `pantsir.py` nor `validate-parts.py` generates or checks a direction), `BoresightMode` (zero non-default users), `MunitionProfile.NeutralDensityRatio` (no registered munition sets it), `Aimpoint.OnPart` and `Aimpoint.AtPoint` (zero production call sites). Both landed features were built ahead of their instance, and the mechanism that produced them was an audit's ranked-change table.

### Cross-cutting defects found while auditing

These are not candidates. They are things that are wrong now.

1. **`SimClock` is dead code.** `SimClock.Classify` has zero call sites in `src/`; `DefenceBattery.AbandonFlight` has zero anywhere. The shipped frame hook does `_battery.Update(Math.Min(dtSim, Interceptor.MaxFaithfulStep))` — the silent clamp CLAUDE.md names explicitly as the old, wrong behaviour. `SimClockTests` passes against dead code. Found independently by two auditors. Under warp, time is discarded without announcement and rounds fall behind the world.
2. **The cannon are gated on the missile pods.** Confirmed under adversarial review. `UpdateGunFireControl` requires `IsLaid`, which resolves `drivesAccepted` from `DriveStatus.AimingAccepted = Works(Turret) && Works(Pods)`. `DriveStatus`'s own comment says "the channels do not share a latch" — they do not share the latch, but the gun's *release* reads the pods'. The reachable facet needs no engine refusal at all: `assembliesResolved` silences a cannon whose `GunsPart` resolved fine whenever the pods subpart does not.
3. **Missiles are released along a tube laid on the gun's ballistic lead**, in the 1200–4000 m overlap where both weapons are in envelope. Confirmed: `AimPointEcl` swings the ring onto the lead whenever `GunsHaveTheEngagement`, the missile ladder never consults that predicate, `IsLaid` only checks the drives settled on *the commanded* bearing, and `LaunchAlongTube` is true. ~18° off for a 300 m/s crosser, roughly range-independent. PN recovers, which is why nothing has measured it. **Computed, not observed.** The fix is one arbitration condition, not per-channel drives.
4. **`MissDistance == 0` kill hazard.** `Detonate` judges the intended-target kill on `round.MissDistance <= lethalRange`; `MissDistance` is written only inside the proximity block and defaults to 0. Latent today — no shipped path detonates by another route — and live the instant a timer or environmental fuse exists. Whoever builds one must set `MissDistance` at the burst or gate the kill on the fuse having fired.
5. **`Detonate`'s comment contradicts its guard.** The comment says a round aimed at a part or coordinate "arrives, reports, and destroys nothing"; the guard is `Kind != Vehicle && Handle is not null`, and `Aimpoint.AtPoint` sets `Handle` to null. A coordinate round runs the full blast sweep. That is the behaviour you want — fix the comment before someone relies on either reading.
6. **`BallisticLead` ignores drag while the shell it fires does not.** Integrating the shipped constants: 4.260 s to 4 km against the solver's assumed 4.167 s. 93 ms is 28 m of target motion for a 300 m/s crosser, against a 3 m fuse radius. A live accuracy limit on the existing 2A38M.
7. **`Arsenal.Named` falls back to element zero silently.** Two munitions are registered now, so a typo in `GunMunition` fires 45 m/s SAMs with a 20 m lethal radius out of the 30 mm barrel.
8. **The manual FIRE button bypasses** `Config.MissilesEnabled`, `InEngagementEnvelope` and `HasSalvoCapacity` — each consulted at exactly one site. The "Missiles" tickbox does not inhibit the FIRE button nine lines below it. Intended override or oversight is unrecorded.
9. **The gun/missile envelopes claim a partition they do not hold.** Profile comments say the cannon cover "what the missiles cannot reach" with "no gap"; the constants are 200–4000 and 1200–20000.
10. **`Sim/Reticle.cs:13` claims the ladder "reads as range".** Both tick position and length are pure functions of `box`, with no range input.
11. **Nine doubled `/// <summary>` blocks** put a member's documentation on the next member and leave the intended one undocumented. `check-comments.sh` does not detect this class.
12. **`validate-parts.py` and `checkswept.py` are single-launcher by construction** — first-match regexes over the whole of `Arsenal.cs`, against one flat `muzzles.json`. A second launcher's geometry is never checked; building its Blender model overwrites `muzzles.json` and the validator then reports the *first* launcher as stale; and if the new profile is declared above the Pantsir, both checkers validate its numbers against the Pantsir's mesh.

## TRACK 3 — Contributor experience

Ordered by friction removed per unit effort. Silent failures first, because a tool that reports green without checking is worse than no tool.

### Silent — fix these first (minutes each)

1. **`doctor.sh` reports "commit-msg hook installed" in a fresh clone where it is not.** The check is `core.hooksPath == .githooks || -x .githooks/commit-msg`, and the second branch always passes because the file is tracked with its exec bit. Verified end to end: fresh clone, doctor said ok, `git commit -m "wip: this message does not parse"` succeeded. Drop the `-x` fallback. This is the one first-hour failure whose consequence is invisible — a non-parsing message cuts no release and never reaches the changelog.
2. **`api-surface.sh --check` passes against a stale build.** It only builds when the DLL is absent. Verified: added a new KSA dependency, ran the check without rebuilding, got "API surface matches the code". Delete the conditional. An incremental build measured 1.0 s against the check's own 1.04 s — it costs nothing and closes the only path by which running the check can lie. This is precisely how the current HEAD came to exist as a CI repair commit.
3. **`check-assemblies.sh` never prints its remediation block.** `set -e` + `pipefail` kill the script on the `diff` pipeline, so the four-command KSA-update recovery heredoc immediately below is dead code — in both modes, and `build.sh` runs this on every build. Append `|| true` to the pipeline. One token restores the only guidance a contributor gets after a KSA update.
4. **`validate-parts.py` accepts unknown flags silently.** `--ofline` and `--help` both run the online path and exit 0; `--help` prints a full successful validation run. Replace the `in sys.argv` membership test with argparse, matching `checkswept.py`.
5. **A second `LauncherProfile` passes every gate while naming a part Id and markers that exist nowhere in the XML.** Verified: added one, `validate-parts.py --offline` said OK, 353 tests passed, boundary and comment checks clean. Nothing compares `LauncherProfile.PartId` to a `<Part Id=…>`. This is the documented extension path with no gate on it.
6. **Fork CI reports green having compiled and tested nothing** — every step of the `build` job is secret-gated. README tells fork contributors the *opposite* of what happens: it says a fork gets `test.sh` and `check-boundary.sh` and not the asset checks; in fact the `tooling` job is ungated and runs everything except the build and the C# tests. Combined with the next item, an outside contributor's guidance change is verified by nobody anywhere.

### Wrong promises and missing wiring (small)

7. **"A large amount of this repository is testable with nothing but the .NET SDK" is false.** The test csproj references `Brutal.Core.Numerics.dll` and `Brutal.Core.Common.dll`; with no assemblies, `test.sh` errors out. You cannot contribute to `Sim/` without owning KSA. Fix the sentence in CONTRIBUTING and README. Making it *true* is large — `double3`/`float3` appears 206 times across 16 of 26 files under `Sim/`.
8. **`StarMap.API.dll` does not ship with KSA, but `HaveKsaAssemblies` only tests for `KSA.dll`.** Anyone resolving to a bare game install — including via CONTRIBUTING's own documented `KSA_DLL_DIR=/path/to/Kitten Space Agency ./tools/build.sh` — gets 13 × CS0246 about `StarMap`, the exact wall the friendly Error target exists to prevent, while doctor.sh reports the assemblies fine. Add it to the condition and name it in the error text. Point CONTRIBUTING's example at `sync-import.sh`, the only path that gets both halves.
9. **`tools/check-all.sh`, and a `pre-push` hook that runs it.** Three "before you open a PR" lists exist (CONTRIBUTING 5 items, README 4 different items, CLAUDE.md a menu) against CI's 16 steps, and two CI steps exist only as inline YAML and cannot be run locally at all. Measured: the entire non-`checkswept` set is ~7 s. `core.hooksPath` is already wired to a tracked `.githooks/`, so a `pre-push` arrives with a pull for everyone who ran `install-hooks.sh`. Then have `ci.yml` call the same script, so the two cannot drift — the one-script-two-callers discipline `check-commit-msg.sh` already uses.
10. **`validate-parts.py` never checks XML → markers.** It iterates a hardcoded 5-tuple of marker fields and finds the matching `<SubPart>`; a subpart present in the XML with no marker naming it and nothing driving its transform produces no output at all. ~12 lines in a function that already parses both sides. Source the parent relationships from `checkswept.py:588-592`, which already encodes the articulation graph — do not write a third hand-maintained list.
11. **No check runs in the deploy loop.** Neither `deploy.sh` nor `run.sh` invokes `validate-parts.py` (89 ms), `checkmesh.py` or `checkswept.py`. Run validate-parts after the build and before the copy, and fail the deploy — it converts a wasted launch-inspect-quit cycle into a line of stderr.
12. **`checkswept.py` fires only in CI.** `tools/model/build.sh` runs `checkmesh.py` and not `checkswept.py`, so a sweep-through defect — the class five commits have been repairs for — is only caught after a push. 43 s, but a headless Blender run has already been spent by that point.

## TRACK 4 — AI working agreement

Four mistakes, two underlying failures.

### #1 Stale API surface — and #3 undriven optical head: **the gate sits after the round trip**

The stale surface was only ever asked about by CI, i.e. after the push. The undriven subpart was only ever observable in game, i.e. after the deploy. In both cases a local check costing under a second either already existed or was a dozen lines from existing, and nothing ran it at the moment it would have paid.

**#1 mechanism:** delete the `if [[ ! -f "$MOD_DLL" ]]` guard in `api-surface.sh` so it always builds (1.0 s), add `.githooks/pre-push` running it when `src/**/*.cs` differs from upstream, and add the one line to CONTRIBUTING's pre-PR list so the human-readable list and the enforced list agree. Skip cleanly with a warning when the assemblies are absent, so a fork is not blocked.

**#3 mechanism:** `validate-parts.py` gains the reverse check — every `<SubPart Id=…>` under `<Part>` must be named by a marker in `Arsenal.cs` or appear in a literal allowlist of deliberately-static bodies. Source the parent graph from `checkswept.py:588-592`. A body whose parent is not the chassis and which nothing writes each frame will lag its parent, and that becomes detectable in 89 ms without the game. Then run it from `deploy.sh` before the copy.

A tempting third grouping — #2 and #3 both burned deploy cycles — is a shared *symptom*. #3 was answerable statically with no deploy at all; #2 needed exactly one.

### #2 Camera write assumed — and #4 relative-velocity assumed: **a contract at a boundary was assumed instead of established**

#2 assumed an engine contract ("KSA honours a mod's camera write"); #4 assumed a call contract ("`targetVelocity` means relative"). Both were answerable cheaply and up front, and both deferred the answer behind work that depended on it.

**#2 mechanism — a rule with two artefacts, both of which have homes.** The rule: *before building on a write the engine has never been seen to honour, ship the smallest thing that answers whether it does, deploy it alone, and log the answer.* The trigger is a design-time yes/no question — "does this depend on a `KsaWorld` write with no prior in-game confirmation?" The artefacts: a row in `CHECKLIST.md`'s risk table stating the unknown before the work starts, and — once answered — either a `docs/BLOCKED-ON-KSA.md` entry or a `…Works` capability flag with a fallback. The precedent already exists and was not used: `DefenceBattery.RoundBodiesWork`, a flag defaulting true, cleared when a write is refused, with the gizmo tracers kept as fallback.

Also delete CLAUDE.md's line that the subpart transform write "was the last big 'the API allows it, does the engine agree' unknown, and the answer is yes." It was untrue the moment the camera write was contemplated, and it retires a question that must stay open while KSA is pre-release. Replace with the general form: *whether the engine honours a write is a separate question from whether the API exposes one, and it has been answered separately every time.*

**#4 mechanism — change the signature so the wrong value cannot be passed.** `BallisticLead.TrySolve` still takes a pre-differenced velocity, so the subtraction carrying the whole frame contract lives at a call site in `Ksa/`, where no test reaches it. Renaming the parameter fixed the instance and left the class open. Take both terms and difference them inside: `TrySolve(shooterPosEcl, shooterVelocityEcl, targetPosEcl, targetVelocityEcl, …)`. There is then no argument that means "relative". This is the shape the rest of `Sim/` already uses — `Interceptor.Update` takes `platformEcl` and computes the offset itself. Generalise it: **a `Sim/` entry point takes both frame-carrying terms and differences them itself; it never accepts a difference computed in `Ksa/`.** The four hardest bugs in `FRAMES-AND-EPOCHS.md` are all differences taken at the wrong place or instant.

And replace the regression test. `CommonMotionMustNotBeLedOn` asserts the *solver* is sensitive to a 29.8 km/s common term; the bug was at the call site, and the test would have passed unchanged against it. That is the fourth time "a regression test only counts if it fails against the old code" has been broken. After the signature change, assert *invariance*: add the same arbitrary velocity to both inputs and assert the aim point is unchanged to within a metre. Keep the sensitivity assertion as the companion — one proves the term matters, the other proves it is removed.

A frame-tag naming convention (`vel` identifiers must end in `Ecl` / `Local` / `Relative` / `PartFrame` / …, enforced by `check-comments.sh`) is worth having only as the cheap net under call sites that must take a pre-differenced value for other reasons. It catches the naming, not the value. About ten offenders in `src/` today.

**`docs/FRAMES-AND-EPOCHS.md` did not prevent #4 and could not have.** It is a document about diagnosing frame bugs after they manifest; #4's bug was a parameter name at a call site in `Ksa/`, which no `Sim/` test can reach.

### The structural observation

CLAUDE.md is a good working agreement — invariants with the measurement attached, why rather than what, and an unusual amount of it backed by a real tool wired into CI. The failures cluster in one place: **the rule it states most emphatically — "Docs are part of the change, not a follow-up" — is the one major rule with no tool behind it, and it is the one that broke.**

Two more, both cheap:

- **The hardest rule ships with no procedure.** "Do not commit a behaviour fix as a fix until verified in game" gets three paragraphs of justification and zero commands, while every component of a procedure exists and is scattered across four files. Write a `verify-in-game` skill: deploy, attach, turn on Verbose and the diagnostic dump, spawn a target, and the exact grep over `KSArmory.log` that distinguishes the four failure shapes — with an explicit "hand back to the human here, the panel needs a mouse" step, and a closing step that writes the result into `CHECKLIST.md`. This is the highest-value skill the repo does not have, because it is attached to the rule the repo says has cost it the most.
- **130 lines of Blender rules sit in the file loaded at session start**, and there is no nested `CLAUDE.md` anywhere. `tools/model/README.md` already covers the same ground and the two have diverged. Move the non-negotiables to `tools/model/CLAUDE.md`, where they load at the moment the agent touches those files, and cut the root file to a pointer. Same treatment for the frame/epoch material, which is duplicated in full from `FRAMES-AND-EPOCHS.md` — keep only the one-line invariant and the `DrawAnchor` two-instants warning, which is the trap an agent walks into while tidying without having gone looking for frame docs.
